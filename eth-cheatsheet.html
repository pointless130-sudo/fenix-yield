Where Are You Sending Your ETH?

Wallet & app network cheatsheet · Updated July 2026 · Check this before you withdraw

⚠️ Sending to the wrong network can mean permanent loss of funds
Your Fenix vault lives on Ethereum Mainnet, and every withdrawal it sends is a Mainnet transaction. Many popular wallets and exchange deposit addresses now default to a Layer 2 network (Base, Arbitrum, Optimism, Polygon…). An address on the wrong network can look identical — but ETH sent to an address that only exists on a different network may be unrecoverable. No one — not Fenix, not the wallet company — can reverse a blockchain transaction.
Before you hit Send — 3 checks
1. Find your destination wallet/app in the table below and check its Primary Network.
2. In the destination app, make sure the receive address you copied is for Ethereum Mainnet (often shown as "Ethereum", "ETH network", or "ERC-20" — not Base, Arbitrum, Optimism or Polygon).
3. First time sending to a new address? Send a small test amount first, confirm it arrives, then send the rest.

Good to know:
• Custodial apps (Venmo, Robinhood, Fidelity…) hold your crypto for you — you don't control the private keys.
• Non-custodial wallets (and your Fenix vault) give you full control of your funds.
• Amber rows default to — or prominently offer — a Layer 2 network. In those apps, double-check you've selected an Ethereum Mainnet receive address before withdrawing from Fenix.
• Robinhood launched its own Layer 2 (Robinhood Chain, built on Arbitrum) in July 2026 — when depositing to Robinhood, explicitly choose the Ethereum network, not Robinhood Chain or Arbitrum.
• Exchange deposit addresses often support multiple networks — always pick Ethereum (ERC-20) when depositing from Fenix.
• Apps add new networks frequently — when in doubt, check the network label on the deposit screen of the receiving app. It always says which network the address is for.
